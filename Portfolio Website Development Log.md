# Portfolio Website Development Log

## Approach 
Before starting to create the website, I looked into what would be showcased on my portfolio. I want my portfolio to be primarily focused on games programming but I didn't know if I had enough work to just have my programming projects on the site. So I looked back at my old game design work and found some 3D models I could also add in as well as some animation work that could be added. I also put some thought into what sections I would want on my portfolio which lead me to adding in a skills section as well as a section for external links to my Itch.io account and my Github account. <br>

## Result 
A lot of things changed and were added during the creation of my website. For one, I added an education section to it that displayed my degrees, grades and what I learnt while working towards that degree. This was one of the last things I added as I took inspiration for it during presentations of our sites. One thing I did not end up adding was my animation work. Personally I felt that adding that as well as 3D modelling would make my portfolio look like it was very generalised and not targeted at a specific job role, so I just kept in my projects and 3D models; keeping the 3D models below the projects to make sure they are not the focus. <br>
I also made sure that everything was easy to access, adding links all over that page: to my Github account, to my Itch account, to each project's repository and links to play and view each of the projects. I was also going to add in Sketchfab links for viewing all of the models but I ended up commenting them out for now as I was unable to access my account but I will definitely look into adding that back later. <br>
The part that I didn't imagine would take as long as it did was coming up with the colour scheme. I tested at least ten different colour schemes before landing on the one I did and I get the feeling that I am going to continue changing it until it is perfect in the future.

## Reflection
Overall, creating this portfolio was a great learning experience. It forced me to think about not just writing code, but how I present my skills and work to future employers. Finding the right look for the site took a bit of tweaking, especially when it came to getting the colour themes right so that the text was actually easy to read while keeping a dark theme that suits game developers. <br>
Setting up the Doxygen documentation alongside Github Actions was definitely the most technical part of the project,
